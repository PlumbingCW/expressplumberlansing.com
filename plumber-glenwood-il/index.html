Along the Glenwood Shoreline

Wikipedia notes that the Glenwood Shoreline, an ancient shoreline of Lake Chicago, runs through the village. Ground levels change across that ridge, so two basements a few blocks apart can drain very differently.

If yours stays damp, check the sump pit and gutters first. Our basement waterproofing page explains how we find the source.

Near the Forest Preserves

Forest preserves nearly surround Glenwood, which means tree roots are a common cause of slow drains on lots that border them. Roots seek out small leaks in clay and cast iron pipe joints.

A camera inspection shows whether roots are in your line, and sewer camera inspection explains what we check.

Along Halsted Street and the Village Center

Pace routes 352 and 890 run along Halsted Street, the village's main commercial corridor, where businesses and apartments depend on shared drain lines. Commercial calls are common near that stretch. See commercial plumbing for what we handle.

Residential streets away from Halsted are mostly single-family. Glenwood's population peaked at 10,538 in 1980 and is 8,662 now, so many homes are from the decades before 1980.

Churches, Schools and Shared Buildings

Glenwood has churches, elementary schools and a private academy, and buildings like these have large restrooms, kitchens and water heaters that behave like commercial systems. A failed heater or a stopped main line in a building with many users is urgent.

If you manage a building like this, give us the contact for after-hours access when you call.
