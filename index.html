Lansing Plumbing: What Our Streets and Homes Ask of Us

Lansing sits on the Illinois and Indiana line, with Hammond to the northeast and Munster to the east. The village buys Lake Michigan water from Hammond, according to a 2025 University of Illinois Chicago water affordability study, and village staff quoted in it say many mains are now 40 to 60 years old, with frequent main breaks. A sudden pressure drop or a brown tap after a main break is worth a call, because debris can clog aerators and fixtures.

That study also reports that 39 percent of Lansing homes date from 1959 or earlier, with much of the older housing built with lead plumbing. If your house is from that era, ask us about water line repair and replacement when you see rust, low pressure or repeated leaks near the meter.

The Little Calumet River forms the village's northern edge. A restoration plan for the levee describes about 1.25 miles of levee in Lansing, and northeast Lansing flooded in 2008 after a levee failure near the Munster border. Homes near the river should treat a working sump pump as basic equipment, and sump pump repair is a call we take seriously after any storm.

We work along Ridge Road, Torrence Avenue and Bernice Road, and around Lansing Woods, Lan-Oak Park and downtown Lansing. We also serve South Holland, Thornton, Lynwood, Glenwood and Calumet City.
