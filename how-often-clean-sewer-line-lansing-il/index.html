How Often Should a Sewer Line Be Cleaned in Lansing, IL?

Most homes with no clog history do not need a routine sewer cleaning, but a house with older pipe, big trees or repeated backups benefits from a camera check or cleaning every year or two. The right interval depends on what is in the pipe, not on a fixed date.

This guide explains how to judge your own line, what warning signs to watch and what a plumber does during a cleaning. It is written for homeowners in Lansing, IL.

Why There Is No Single Answer

A sewer line is a pipe from your house to the street. A new plastic line with no trees nearby can run for many years with no trouble, while an old clay line with roots in the joints can clog within months.

Instead of a calendar, think about risk. Pipe age, material, trees, how many people live in the house and what goes down the drains all change how fast a line fills.

Warning Signs That the Line Needs Attention

Catch these early and cleaning stays simple.

Gurgling from drains

Air is being pushed back through traps.

Slow drains in several fixtures

The blockage is likely in the main line.

Sewer odor in the basement

Gas may be escaping through a dry trap or a poor seal.

A floor drain that bubbles when the washer runs

The main line is restricted.

Cable or Jet at a Scheduled Cleaning

A plumber chooses based on what the camera shows.

Cable cleaning

  • Good for a localized blockage
  • Cuts through roots in a narrow area
  • Lower cost per visit

Hydro jetting

  • Scours the whole pipe wall
  • Removes grease and scale buildup
  • A longer-lasting result on lines with heavy buildup. See hydro jetting

What Roots Actually Do to a Pipe

Roots do not break healthy pipe. They follow moisture to a small crack or a loose joint, enter as thin hairs and then thicken into a mat that catches paper and grease. Over time the mat narrows the pipe and holds back flow.

That is why roots come back. A cutter clears the mat but the entry point remains, so the plumber may suggest a camera visit, a liner or a spot repair to close it.

How to Check Your Line Between Visits

Once a month, run water in the tub or the washer and listen at the floor drain. Gurgling, slow draining or a smell in the basement are signs the line is filling.

Keep a simple log with dates and symptoms. If you call a plumber, the log helps to show whether trouble is getting more frequent, which points to a cause that cleaning will not remove.

What a Cleaning Visit Looks Like

The plumber finds the cleanout, runs a cable or jet through the line and then pulls a camera through to check the result. You see the pipe before and after, which tells you whether cleaning was enough.

If the camera shows a broken or collapsed section, cleaning will not hold and repair is the right next step. Read about sewer line repair for the options, and see how nearby towns such as Lynwood and South Holland handle the same problem.
