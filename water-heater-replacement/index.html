A Failed Tank Floods Fast: What to Do While You Wait

Shut the cold water valve above the heater and switch off the gas or the breaker. Open a hot tap upstairs to relieve pressure if the tank is still hissing. Then move anything stored near the heater and call the 24/7 line.

Water at the base of a tank is usually the end of its life. A leak that starts small can release a full tank onto a basement floor, so do not wait to see if it stops.

What Replacement Day Looks Like

A replacement is a planned job even when the call was an emergency. The steps are below.

1

Drain and disconnect

The plumber shuts off fuel and water, drains the old tank and disconnects the lines.

2

Remove and haul

The old heater leaves with the plumber, so you are not stuck with a dead tank in the basement.

3

Set and connect

The new unit is leveled, piped, vented and fitted with a new relief valve and drain line as needed.

4

Fill, fire and test

The tank is filled before heat is applied, then the plumber checks for leaks, venting and temperature.

Choosing the Replacement: Tank, Tankless or Hybrid

The best unit depends on household size, fuel and space. This is a general guide, and the plumber can narrow it down at your home.

OptionGood fit forThings to weigh
Standard tankMost households and tight budgetsUses stored hot water, so a long shower can run it down
TanklessHomes that want endless hot water and floor space backNeeds the right gas or electric supply. See tankless water heaters
Heat pump hybridElectric homes with open space around the unitNeeds room and air flow, and a drain for condensate

Replacing a heater is also a good time to look at the gas line, venting and the expansion tank, which protects fittings from pressure swings.

How Old Is Too Old?

A 2025 study of the Village of Lansing found that 39 percent of local homes date from 1959 or earlier. Many of those houses have had several water heaters, and an older house is more likely to have outdated venting or an undersized gas line that needs attention when a unit is swapped.

Read the label on your tank for the manufacture date. If it is past ten years and starting to leak, rust or rumble, replacement usually costs less than repeated repairs. For a planned swap, read about water heater installation in a home that has never had one moved.
