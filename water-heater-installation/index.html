What a Good Installation Includes

A heater is only as safe as its connections. A proper install means correct pipe sizing, a shutoff valve on the cold supply, a relief valve piped to a safe drain and venting that carries exhaust outside.

The plumber also checks the fuel line, the electrical supply and the floor under the unit. If a heater is moving to a new spot, those details matter even more because the old setup no longer applies.

Installation Jobs We Take On

Most installs fall into one of these groups.

  • ✓
    New construction and additionsA new bathroom or basement suite often needs a second heater or a larger unit.
  • ✓

  • ✓

  • ✓
    Adding an expansion tankA closed water system can build pressure when water heats, and an expansion tank gives it room.

Getting Ready for Installation Day

A little preparation shortens the visit and avoids surprises.

1

Clear the area

Move boxes and stored items at least a few feet back from the old and new heater locations.

2

Find the shutoffs

Know where the main water valve and the gas shutoff are, and tell the plumber at the start.

3

Plan for a short outage

Hot water is off while the old unit is drained and the new one is filled and heated.

4

Ask about the old unit

We haul the old heater away, so there is nothing for you to arrange.

Planning Around Lansing's Older Houses

The Lansing Journal's history of the village describes a postwar building boom in the late 1940s and early 1950s, and a 2025 study found that 39 percent of homes date from 1959 or earlier. Those houses were often built with small utility closets and basements with low ceilings.

That affects an install. A new heater may need a different vent route or a raised platform in a basement that has taken on water, and we plan for it during the visit.
