Waterproof Membrane for Basements in Lansing, IL

A waterproof membrane is a barrier applied to a basement wall or laid in a drainage system to keep water from passing through. It helps against dampness and seepage, but it does not remove the groundwater pushing against the wall, so it works best with drainage and a sump pump.

This guide explains the types of membranes, what each can and cannot do and how to decide for your home. It is written for homeowners in Lansing, IL.

What a Membrane Does

A membrane is a layer that blocks water or channels it. A coating or sheet on the outside of a foundation wall keeps water from soaking in, while a dimpled sheet on the inside collects seepage and guides it to a drain.

Both types treat symptoms of water pressure. They are useful, but if groundwater is the cause, the water still has to go somewhere, which is why drainage is part of the plan.

Types of Membranes

Where the membrane goes changes how it works.

TypeWhereWhat it does
Exterior coating or sheetOutside the foundation wallBlocks water from soaking into the wall. Needs excavation
Interior dimpled membraneInside against the wallChannels seepage down to an interior drain
Under-slab vapor barrierUnder the floorReduces moisture rising from the soil
Wall sealer or paintInside surfaceReduces dampness but does not stop water pressure

How an Interior Membrane System Is Installed

The work follows the water path.

1

Open a channel

A strip of floor along the wall is opened for a drain.

2

Install the drain

A perimeter drain collects water and leads it to a sump pit.

3

Attach the membrane

The sheet runs up the wall and into the drain.

4

Connect the pump

A sump pump moves the water out of the house.

Water Pressure Is the Real Opponent

Groundwater presses on a basement wall and floor the way water presses on a dam. The deeper the water table rises, the greater the pressure, and water finds any crack, joint or pore to escape through.

A membrane makes the wall harder to penetrate or gives the water a path to a drain. It is not a plug that holds the pressure, which is why the best plans pair a membrane with drainage and a pump.

Questions to Ask a Contractor

Ask where the water is coming from, what the plan does with it and how the pump will be powered during an outage. A plan that only seals the wall without a place for the water to go is a warning sign.

Ask what the warranty covers, who handles repairs and whether the work is transferable if you sell. Get the answers in writing, along with a clear price before work starts.

What to Do If Water Is Already Coming In

A membrane is a long-term fix. If water is entering now, start here.

  • ✓
    Stay away from electrical itemsDo not stand in water near outlets, cords or the panel. Turn power off only if you can reach the switch while dry.
  • ✓
    Move belongings up and outLift boxes and furniture off the floor and take valuables upstairs.
  • ✓
    Check the sump pumpIf you have one, make sure it is running and the discharge line is clear.
  • ✓
    Photograph the damageTake dated photos of walls, floor and belongings before you clean up.

The Lansing Journal reported on a roughly $15.6 million levee restoration plan for the Little Calumet River, which forms Lansing's northern border. The plan covers about 1.25 miles of levee in Lansing, protects an estimated 2,500 residential and 20 business structures across the project area and notes that northeast Lansing flooded in 2008 after a levee failure near the Munster border.

Homes near the river deal with higher water tables during wet seasons. A membrane alone may not be enough there, and a sump pump with a battery backup is a good partner. See basement waterproofing for how we plan it.

What a Membrane Cannot Fix

A membrane does not fix a leaking pipe, a failed window well or a sewer backup. If water appears after a meter shows no use, call for leak detection before spending on waterproofing.

A membrane also does not stop water that comes up through a floor crack under pressure. That needs drainage and often a pump.

Planning Around a Finished Basement

In a finished basement, an interior system requires removing part of the floor and the lower wall finish. Plan for that cost and the mess, and ask the plumber how restoration is handled.

Permit rules vary, so ask the Village of Lansing Building Department what applies to waterproofing work. Our Glenwood callers often ask about the order of work too.
