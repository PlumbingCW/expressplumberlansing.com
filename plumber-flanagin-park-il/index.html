Plumber in Flanagin Park, Lansing, IL

Flanagin Park is a subdivision in Lansing, Illinois, just off Wentworth Avenue. Homeowners here may need a plumber for a failed sump pump, a slow drain or a wet basement.

The neighborhood has a documented flooding history, so most of what follows is about water below the floor and what to do about it.

Why Flanagin Park Is Called a Bowl

The Daily Southtown reported in July 2024 that dozens of homes in the Flanagin Park subdivision sit at one of the village's lowest spots, and officials call the area the Flanagin Bowl. A Lansing Journal report adds that village staff described some of these homes as being at the lowest elevation in the entire village.

Village officials met with residents in July 2018 about repeated flooding. A corrugated steel culvert that drained the subdivision was partly filled with silt and failing in places, and at times water rose high enough to enter basements.

For a homeowner, low ground means groundwater and storm runoff reach your basement first. A sump pump that works is the first thing to check, and our sump pump repair page explains what a visit covers.

The North Creek Flood Relief Project: What Changed and What Did Not

The Village and the Metropolitan Water Reclamation District held a ribbon cutting on July 30, 2024 for the North Creek Flood Relief Project at Flanagin Park, on Sherman Street near 188th Street. The $1.8 million project replaced a steel culvert with a concrete one, and the District's Local Stormwater Partnership Program paid for most of it.

Officials expect it to reduce flood risk for 56 residential structures. They also call it a first phase, with more detention and rerouting of stormwater still being considered, so a new culvert does not remove the need for a working pump.

We cannot tell you whether your house is one of the 56. The Village can say which properties benefit, so ask Village Hall before you decide how much basement protection you need.

Before the Plumber Arrives: A Wet Basement Checklist

These steps are safe for most homeowners and make the visit faster.

Stay clear of standing water

Do not walk through water near outlets, the panel or plugged-in appliances.

Look at the sump pit

Check whether the pump is running, whether the float moves freely and whether the discharge pipe outside is clear.

Lift what you can

Move boxes, electronics and stored items off the floor before more water arrives.

Take photos first

Photograph the water line and damaged items before you move them, in case you file an insurance claim.

Drainage Beyond Your Property Line

The Daily Southtown says water from the subdivision flows generally south through ditches to the Burnham and Lincoln-Lansing drainage ditches. In intense storms the Burnham ditch can take in water from Lynwood, Sauk Village and Munster, Indiana.

A plumber can fix what is inside your property, but street drainage and ditches belong to the Village and the District. If you see standing water in the street or a blocked culvert, report it to the Village so the right crew handles it.
