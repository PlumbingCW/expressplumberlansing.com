Where Oak Glen Is, and Why Its Boundaries Are Historical

The Lansing Journal reports that the community took the name Oak Glen on September 10, 1893, and was incorporated into Lansing that same year. Its historical limits ran roughly from 178th Street on the north to 186th Street on the south, and from School Street on the east to the town of Thornton on the west.

Those are historical limits, not a current official map. If you are not sure that your street counts as Oak Glen, call and we will talk through your address. Our Thornton page covers the village on Oak Glen's western edge.

What the Area's History Means for Your Plumbing Today

The same history says the land was waterlogged with no outlets until ditches were dug in 1862 and 1863, and a county canal connection in 1868 lowered the water about ten feet. That does not tell us anything about your pipes, but it does mean groundwater is worth checking, and sump pump repair is the place to start if your basement gets damp.

Housing age varies across a district that has existed for well over a century. Look up your home's year built on the county property record, then check the pipe where the water line enters the house. A plumber can identify the material in a short visit.

Water Lines, Leaks and the Village Records

Lansing buys Lake Michigan water from Hammond, according to a 2025 University of Illinois Chicago affordability study. We do not quote hardness or lead figures for Oak Glen because the Village's own water report is the right source.

Ask the Village for its latest water report and whether it records the material of your service line. If your inspection finds an old line, water line repair and replacement and repiping are the options we would explain. When a bill rises with no visible cause, leak detection finds the source without opening walls first.

Before the Plumber Visits

A little preparation shortens the visit.

Find the main shutoff

Know where the main water valve is and make sure it turns.

Note when it started

Write down which fixtures are affected and when you first noticed the problem.

Read the meter

With every tap off, check whether the meter dial still moves. A moving dial points to a leak.

Clear the way

Move items away from the water heater, the main shutoff and the area where the line enters the house.
