Toilet Overflowing? Stop the Water

Lift the tank lid and push the flapper down, or turn the water supply valve behind the toilet clockwise to stop refilling. Do not flush again.

If other fixtures are slow or gurgling, the blockage may be in the main line. Stop using water and call the 24/7 line. See drain cleaning for what a plumber does with a line that is backing up.

Toilet Problems and What They Mean

Different symptoms point to different parts.

Runs after flushing

A worn flapper or fill valve lets water keep moving, wasting water and raising your bill.

Weak or slow flush

Clogged rim jets or a partly blocked trap reduce flush force.

Water around the base

A failed wax ring or a loose bolt lets water seep out.

Rocks on the floor

A loose or damaged flange can move the toilet and break the seal.

Replacing a Toilet the Right Way

A new toilet is more than swapping a bowl.

1

Shut off and drain

The water supply is closed and the tank and bowl are emptied.

2

Remove and inspect

The old toilet comes off and the flange is checked for damage.

3

Set with a new seal

A new wax ring or seal and fresh bolts are used.

4

Test and level

The plumber checks for a stable, level fixture and no leaks.

Why a Running Toilet Is Not Harmless

A toilet that runs after each flush can waste a large amount of water over a month without any sign except a higher bill. A quick check is to drop a few drops of food coloring into the tank and see if color shows in the bowl without flushing.

If it does, the flapper is leaking. Replacing the flapper or fill valve is one of the cheapest plumbing repairs, and a plumber can do it while checking the shutoff valve and supply line for wear.

What to Do Before We Arrive for a Toilet Problem

A few quick steps keep a toilet problem contained.

When the Floor Is the Problem

A toilet that keeps rocking or leaking at the base may have a damaged flange or a soft floor from a slow leak. Many Lansing houses were built in the postwar years, and a leak that has run for a long time can rot the subfloor.

If you notice a soft spot near the toilet, tell the plumber. Replacing a flange and a section of subfloor costs less than waiting until the toilet falls through.
