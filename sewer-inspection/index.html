What the Camera Shows

A camera head with lights travels down the line while the plumber watches a monitor. It shows the pipe material, joints, obstructions and the shape of the pipe.

A locator on the camera lets the plumber mark the exact depth and position at ground level, so a repair is dug in the right place and not a few feet off.

Reading the Footage With Your Plumber

The video shows the pipe interior as the camera moves, and the plumber narrates what to look for. Dark, smooth pipe walls point to clean flow, while a rough or flaky surface suggests scale or corrosion.

A pipe that sags will show a pool of standing water, often called a belly. Cracks and offset joints appear as breaks in the line of the wall. Ask the plumber to pause on each problem so you can see it for yourself and ask questions.

Older Homes and Mixed Pipe Materials

A 2025 study found that 39 percent of homes in Lansing were built in 1959 or earlier, and the Lansing Journal describes a postwar boom in the late 1940s and early 1950s. Houses from those years may have clay, cast iron or other older sewer pipe, sometimes joined to newer plastic sections.

Where old and new pipe meet, offsets and root entry are common. A video inspection is the easiest way to learn what you own before a problem forces the issue.
