Will Coconut Oil Clog Drains in Lansing, IL?

Yes, coconut oil can clog drains. It is liquid when warm and turns solid at cooler temperatures, so it can harden inside a cold drain pipe and build up with other grease and food scraps. Pouring it down the sink, even with hot water, only moves the problem farther down the line.

This guide explains why coconut oil is a drain problem, what to do with it instead and what to do if your sink is already slow. It is written for homeowners in Lansing, IL.

Why Coconut Oil Solidifies in a Pipe

Coconut oil is mostly saturated fat, which stays liquid at warm temperatures and solidifies at room temperature and below. Hot water melts it in the sink, but the oil cools as it travels into colder pipe and sets again.

The solid fat sticks to the pipe wall and catches hair, food bits and soap. Layer after layer narrows the pipe until the sink slows and then stops.

Fats and Oils and Where They Belong

Most cooking fats behave the same way in a drain.

What to Do Instead

A simple routine keeps fat out of the pipes.

1

Let it cool

Wait until the fat is cool or solid.

2

Scrape or pour

Scrape solid fat into the trash or pour liquid fat into a container.

3

Wipe the pan

Use a paper towel to wipe grease before washing.

4

Use a strainer

A sink strainer catches food scraps that mix with grease.

Why the Clog Builds Slowly and Then Stops All at Once

A fat clog does not appear overnight. Each time grease or oil goes down the sink, a thin film coats the pipe wall. Soap and food scraps stick to the film and the opening shrinks a little each week.

By the time the sink drains slowly, the pipe may be mostly blocked. The last stage is sudden, when a small piece of food or a hair mat finishes the job and the sink stops. That is why a slow sink deserves attention early, not after it overflows.

Restaurants, Shops and Heavy Use

A home kitchen puts a small amount of fat into the line. A restaurant or shop on a commercial strip puts in much more, and that is why commercial kitchens use grease traps and schedule line cleaning.

If you run a food business in Lansing, ask the Village of Lansing about requirements for grease control, and see commercial plumbing for how we handle floor drains and kitchen lines.

Lansing, IL Note: Fat, Sewers and Neighboring Backups

ABC7 Chicago reported that Calumet City, next to Lansing, is working to replace or repair bad sewers and to seek federal grants for separate storm sewer systems to reduce backups in combined sewers. Neighboring communities have dealt with sewer backups after heavy rain.

Fat and grease reduce the capacity of any sewer, public or private, and they make backups more likely when the system is stressed by storm water. Keeping fat out of your own line is a small way to lower your risk, and our Calumet City and South Holland pages cover nearby areas.

Hot Water, Soap or a Plumber?

Home fixes help in some cases and not in others.

A home fix may help when

  • The sink is just starting to slow
  • The clog is near the drain opening
  • You can reach it with a plunger or a hand auger

Call a plumber when

  • Water does not drain at all
  • Several fixtures are slow
  • The clog returns after you clear it. See drain cleaning

How a Plumber Clears a Fat Clog

A plumber uses a cable to break through the blockage and, for heavy buildup, a water jet to scour the pipe wall. A camera shows how much remains and whether the pipe is damaged.

For a kitchen line that has built up for years, hydro jetting is often the best way to remove the layers. A cable alone may open a hole that closes again within weeks.
