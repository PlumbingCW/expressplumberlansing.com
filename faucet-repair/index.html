Repair or Replace the Faucet?

A new faucet is not always the answer.

Repair works when

  • The faucet is solid and only a part is worn
  • Parts are available
  • The finish and style still suit you

Replace when

  • The body is corroded or leaking from the base
  • Parts are no longer made
  • You want a different style or a touchless model

Kitchen Sinks That Will Not Drain

A sink that holds water is usually blocked in the trap or the line behind it. A plunger and a hand auger clear many, but a blockage past the trap needs a cable.

If the other kitchen fixtures gurgle or the dishwasher backs up, the cause is deeper in the line. A plumber can clear the line and check whether a garbage disposal or a poorly sloped drain is part of the problem.

What to Do Before We Arrive for a Faucet Problem

Most faucet problems are not emergencies, but a few steps prevent damage.

  • ✓
    Close the shutoff valve under the sinkTurn it clockwise until the drip stops. If it will not turn, shut off the main water instead.
  • ✓
    Do not force a stuck handlePrying can crack the fixture or the sink and turn a small repair into a replacement.
  • ✓
    Put a towel and a bowl under the leakThat protects the cabinet floor, where slow leaks cause the most damage.
  • ✓
    Check for wet cabinet wallsSoft or swollen wood means the leak has been running a while. Tell the plumber so they can look at the supply lines.

Older Fixtures in Older Homes

A 2025 study found 39 percent of Lansing homes were built in 1959 or earlier. Original shutoff valves and supply lines in homes that old may be stuck or brittle, so closing them can break them.

Replacing worn angle stops during a faucet job costs little and gives you a shutoff that works the next time you need one. Ask the plumber to check them.
