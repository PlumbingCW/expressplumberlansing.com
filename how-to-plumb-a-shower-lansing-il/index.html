How to Plumb a Shower in Lansing, IL

To plumb a shower, a plumber installs a mixing valve on the hot and cold supply lines, runs a pipe up to the showerhead, sets a drain and trap below the floor and connects the drain to a vent. Each part has to line up with the finished wall and floor, which is why the planning matters as much as the pipe work.

This guide walks through the parts, the order of work and the choices you will face. It is written for homeowners in Lansing, IL planning a new shower or a remodel.

The Four Parts of a Shower System

A shower has a supply side, a valve, a drain side and a vent. Hot and cold water come from the supply lines to the valve, which mixes them and sends water to the showerhead.

Below, the drain collects water in a trap that holds a seal against sewer gas, and the vent lets air in so water drains smoothly.

The Parts, One by One

Knowing the parts helps you read a plumber's quote.

  • ✓
    Mixing valveControls temperature and flow. Pressure balancing and thermostatic types resist sudden temperature swings.
  • ✓
    Supply linesHot and cold pipes run to the valve with shutoffs nearby for future repairs.
  • ✓
    Showerhead armA stub-out pipe in the wall, anchored so it does not move.
  • ✓
    Drain and trapA shower drain and trap connect to the house drain and vent system.

The Order of Work

The sequence matters, since walls close up after the pipes go in.

1

Plan the layout

Choose the valve height, showerhead position and drain location.

2

Rough in the pipes

Supply lines, valve body and drain are set before walls or floors are finished.

3

Pressure test

The supply lines are tested for leaks before anything is covered.

4

Finish and trim

After tile or surround goes up, the trim, showerhead and drain cover are installed.

Pre-made Base or Tiled Pan?

The floor choice changes the drain work.

Pre-made shower base

  • Faster to install and uniform in shape
  • Drain location is set by the base
  • Good for standard sizes

Tiled shower pan

  • Custom size and look
  • Needs a waterproofing layer under the tile
  • More labor and more room for error

Choosing a Shower Valve

A pressure balancing valve keeps the temperature steady when another fixture draws water. A thermostatic valve lets you set a temperature and holds it more precisely, and it costs more.

Pick the valve before the wall is closed, since the rough-in depth depends on the trim kit. Keep the valve model and manual in a drawer, since a repair later needs the exact part.

Questions to Ask a Plumber Before You Start

A short list keeps the job on track.

  • ✓
    Where will the valve go?Height and position should suit everyone who will use the shower.
  • ✓
    What is the drain plan?Ask about trap size, venting and whether the drain location changes.
  • ✓
    Who tests the pan or supply lines?A test before tile prevents the most expensive surprise.
  • ✓
    What access will I have later?An access panel behind the valve makes a future repair far easier.

Mistakes That Cause Shower Leaks Later

Most shower leaks come from the drain connection or from waterproofing, not the valve. A pan that is not tested before tile goes down can leak for months before the damage shows on a ceiling below.

Ask the plumber to pour water into the pan and let it stand before tile. If you plan a basement bath, discuss drain depth too, and our South Holland and Calumet City callers often need a pump for that.
