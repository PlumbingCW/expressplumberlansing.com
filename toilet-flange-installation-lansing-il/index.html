Toilet Flange Installation in Lansing, IL

A toilet flange is the ring that bolts to the floor and connects the toilet to the drain pipe. Installing or replacing one in a Lansing home means setting a new ring at the right height, securing it to a sound floor and sealing the toilet with a fresh wax ring. If your toilet rocks, leaks at the base or smells of sewer gas, a bad flange is a likely cause.

This guide explains how the flange works, how to tell whether yours has failed and when a repair ring is enough. It also covers when to call a plumber.

What a Toilet Flange Does

The flange sits on top of the floor and over the drain opening. Two closet bolts slide into slots on the flange, and the toilet bolts down onto them with a wax ring sealing the joint between the bowl and the pipe.

If the flange is cracked, bent or loose, the toilet cannot sit still and the seal breaks. Water then seeps at the base and, over time, rots the subfloor.

Signs Your Flange Has Failed

A few clues usually appear before a leak does.

The toilet rocks

Movement means the bolts are loose or the flange has shifted or broken.

Water at the base

A wet floor after flushing points to a failed seal, often caused by a damaged flange.

Sewer smell

Gas escaping through a poor seal can smell like sewage.

Soft floor

A spongy spot near the toilet suggests rot from a slow leak.

Repair Ring or Full Replacement?

The damage decides the fix.

Flange conditionUsual fixNotes
Cracked or broken ring, pipe intactRepair ring on topQuick and inexpensive when the floor is sound
Rusted cast iron flangeReplace the flangeRust weakens the bolt slots and the seal
Flange sits below the floor surfaceRaise it with a spacer or new flangeA low flange will not seal well
Subfloor rottedRepair the floor, then set a new flangeA new flange needs solid wood to hold

How a Plumber Replaces a Flange

A typical job follows these steps.

1

Shut off and remove

Water is turned off, the tank and bowl are drained and the toilet is lifted away.

2

Inspect the floor

The plumber checks the subfloor for rot and the pipe for damage.

3

Set the new flange

A new flange is fitted to the pipe and screwed to a sound floor.

4

Reset the toilet

A new wax ring and bolts seal the toilet, which is then tested for leaks.

Flange Materials and Matching the Pipe

Flanges come in PVC, ABS, cast iron, brass and stainless steel rings. The plumber matches the material to the drain pipe, since plastic glues to plastic and cast iron needs a different connection.

A stainless steel ring over a plastic body resists cracking when bolts are tightened, and it is a good choice when the old flange has broken. Cheap thin rings can crack the next time the toilet is reset, so ask what is going in.

Why a Leak at the Base Needs Fast Attention

A leak around a toilet base rarely announces itself. Water runs down between the toilet and the floor and soaks into the subfloor, where it can feed mold and rot the wood. By the time the floor is soft, the repair has grown from a flange to a section of floor.

If you see moisture at the base after a flush, stop using the toilet and call. Our Glenwood and Lynwood callers often ask about this on older homes with original floors.
