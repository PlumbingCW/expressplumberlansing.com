No Heat in Winter: What to Check Safely

Check that the thermostat is calling for heat, the boiler has power and the gas supply is on. If the pressure gauge reads near zero or far above the normal range, do not keep adding water.

Do not try to relight a boiler that has shut off repeatedly, and never ignore a gas smell. Call us, and if the house is very cold, tell dispatch so we can prioritize.

Common Boiler Problems

These are the faults we see most.

Losing pressure

A leak, a failing expansion tank or a faulty relief valve can cause it.

Banging or kettling

Scale or low water flow can make a boiler noisy.

Radiators cold at the top

Trapped air needs bleeding, or the system may need flushing.

Pilot or ignition faults

A dirty burner or failed igniter can stop the boiler.

Repair or Replace the Boiler?

Age and efficiency decide most cases.

Repair when

  • The boiler is relatively young
  • A single part has failed
  • The system has been serviced

Replace when

  • The heat exchanger is cracked or leaking
  • Repairs keep adding up
  • The boiler is very old and inefficient

Boiler Safety Basics

Boilers are pressure vessels and use gas or oil, so safety matters. Install a carbon monoxide alarm near sleeping areas and test it regularly. Keep the area around the boiler clear of storage.

If the relief valve is dripping steadily, the pressure is too high or the valve is failing. Do not cap it. Call a plumber, since a blocked relief valve is a safety hazard.

What to Do Before the Plumber Arrives

Boilers mix heat, pressure and sometimes gas, so a few safety steps matter.

Older Homes, Older Heating

A 2025 study found that 39 percent of Lansing homes were built in 1959 or earlier, and many houses from that era use boilers and radiators. Older systems may have original piping and valves that stick or leak.

An annual service visit catches problems before the first cold snap. Ask the plumber to check the pressure, the expansion tank and the venting each year.
