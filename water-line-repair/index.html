Emergency Water Line Repair in Lansing, IL

Emergency water line repair in Lansing, IL covers the underground pipe that brings water from the street to your house. A licensed plumber finds the leak or break, repairs it or replaces the line and restores pressure. Call our 24/7 line if the yard is flooding, your meter is spinning or pressure has dropped across the house.

A broken service line can waste water fast and wash out soil near the foundation. You get a clear price before work starts.

Yard Flooding or No Water? First Moves

Shut off the main valve inside the house. If water continues to flow in the yard, there may be a shutoff at the street, which belongs to the water utility. Call the village's water office, then call us.

Keep people and pets away from a washed-out area, since soil near a broken line can collapse. Call the 24/7 line and describe where the water is surfacing.

Signs of a Service Line Problem

Underground leaks show in several ways.

Wet or sunken patch

Water surfacing in a lawn between the street and the house suggests a break.

Low pressure everywhere

A narrowed or leaking line reduces pressure at every fixture.

A meter moving with everything off

Water is going somewhere underground.

Rust or discolored water

Aging service lines can shed rust into the water.

Lead Service Lines in an Older Village

A 2025 University of Illinois Chicago study of Lansing's water affordability reports that much of the older housing stock was built with lead plumbing, and that the village is working with the Illinois EPA on low-interest loans to replace lead service lines. The study does not give a count of lead lines.

If your house is from 1959 or earlier, ask the village about your service line material. If a plumber opens your line for another repair, it is a good time to see what the pipe is made of. See repiping for the plumbing inside the house.

Before You Authorize a Dig

Ask the plumber to show you where the line runs, how deep it is and what repair method is proposed. Get a clear price before work starts, and ask what happens to your lawn, driveway or walk afterward.

Call 811 before any digging so buried gas, electric and other utility lines are marked. Your plumber will normally arrange this, but it helps to know it is part of the process.

Mains, Pressure and Your Line

The same study quotes village staff saying many water mains are approaching 40, 50 and 60 years old and that main breaks are frequent. A pressure drop in your house may come from the main, not your line.

Check with a neighbor. If their pressure is also down, call the village. If only your house is affected, call us to check your line.
