Leaking Tank or No Hot Water? Do This First

If water is pooling around the tank, turn off the cold water supply valve on top of the heater and shut off the gas or the electric breaker. Then call the 24/7 line. A tank that leaks from the bottom has usually failed inside and cannot be patched, while a drip from a supply pipe may be a job for leak detection.

If you smell gas, do not touch switches or light anything. Leave the house and call 911 before you call us. A gas smell is never a repair question first.

If there is simply no hot water, check that the breaker has not tripped or that the pilot is lit, then call. Repeated resets point to a failed part, and forcing them can make it worse.

Common Water Heater Faults We Repair

Most failures fall into a handful of patterns. Knowing which one you have helps us bring the right parts on the first visit.

No hot water at all

On electric units the cause is often a failed heating element or thermostat. On gas units it is usually the thermocouple, the igniter or the gas control valve.

Lukewarm water

A broken dip tube, a worn element or a thick layer of sediment can keep the tank from delivering full heat.

Rumbling or popping

Sediment hardens on the tank bottom and traps steam as it heats. Flushing helps early, but heavy buildup can shorten the tank's life.

Water from the relief valve

A valve that drips can mean excess pressure or a failing expansion setup, not just a bad valve. We check the cause before swapping parts.

Repair It or Replace It?

Age and the location of the fault decide most cases. The comparison below is how we usually weigh it with homeowners.

Repair usually makes sense when

  • The tank is not leaking and is under about ten years old
  • A single part such as an element, thermostat or valve has failed
  • The unit has been serviced and flushed through its life

Replacement usually makes sense when

  • Water is leaking from the tank body or seams
  • The heater is old and failing repeatedly, so repair bills keep coming
  • Rust-colored hot water suggests the inside of the tank is corroding. See water heater replacement

Cold Months and Hard Use in Lansing Homes

Lansing buys Lake Michigan water from Hammond, Indiana, and a 2025 water affordability study of the village says many of its mains are 40 to 60 years old. Main breaks and repairs can stir up sediment, which settles in the bottom of a water heater tank.

If your hot water turns cloudy or noisy after a street repair, a flush may be all it needs. Ask the plumber to check the anode rod at the same visit, since it protects the tank from corrosion and is cheaper to replace than the tank.
