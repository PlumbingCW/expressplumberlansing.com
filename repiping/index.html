Signs Your Pipes Are at the End of Their Life

No single sign proves it, but several together point to repiping.

Rust-colored or metallic water

Corroded galvanized or iron pipe sheds rust into the water.

Low pressure on every fixture

Mineral buildup inside old pipe narrows it over time.

Repeated pinhole leaks

A second leak in a short time suggests the whole run is failing.

Visible corrosion or green stains

Corroded joints and fittings on exposed pipe signal age.

Rust, Pressure and What They Say About Your Pipes

Water that runs brown for a few seconds after sitting, then clears, often points to rusting iron or galvanized pipe. Water that stays brown or yellow for a long time suggests heavier corrosion or a problem in the main.

Low pressure at every fixture usually means the supply pipe has narrowed inside. A plumber can check pressure with a gauge and look at the water entering the house to tell a service line problem from an interior one.

Spot Repair or Whole-House Repipe?

The answer depends on how much of the system is failing.

A spot repair fits when

  • One short section has failed
  • The rest of the pipe is in good shape
  • You plan to sell and want a limited fix

A repipe fits when

  • Leaks keep appearing in different places
  • Water is discolored throughout the house
  • Pressure is low at every fixture

Lead and Older Plumbing in Lansing

A 2025 University of Illinois Chicago study of Lansing's water affordability states that much of the village's older housing was built with lead plumbing, and that the village is working with the Illinois EPA on low-interest loans to replace lead service lines. The study does not give a count of lead lines.

If your house was built in 1959 or earlier, ask us about your service line and interior pipes, and see water line repair and replacement. Flush a tap for 30 seconds to two minutes if water has sat for hours, which is the advice in nearby municipal water reports.
