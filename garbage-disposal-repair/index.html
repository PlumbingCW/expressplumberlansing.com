Disposal Jammed or Leaking: Safe First Steps

Turn off the disposal switch and unplug it or cut power at the breaker. Never put your hand inside the chamber, even when it is off. Use a wooden spoon handle to free a jam from above.

If it leaks from the bottom, the seals have likely failed and replacement is usually the answer. Call the 24/7 line.

Why a Disposal Jams in the First Place

Most jams come from a hard object such as a bone, a fruit pit, a bottle cap or a utensil, or from stringy fibers wrapping around the blades. A long period of disuse can also let the parts rust and seize.

Running the disposal now and then keeps the parts moving. If the unit has jammed more than once, the motor or the blades may be wearing out, and a plumber can tell whether a replacement makes more sense than another reset.

What Not to Put Down a Disposal

Grease, coffee grounds, bones, fibrous scraps such as celery and starchy foods such as rice can build up or jam a disposal and the drain behind it. They also feed the buildup that clogs a main line later.

Scrape plates into the trash and run cold water while the unit works. A disposal is for small food scraps, not for everything that comes off a plate.
