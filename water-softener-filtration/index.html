Start With a Test, Not a Guess

Ask your water utility for its annual water quality report and use a home test kit for hardness at your tap. Results vary house to house because of old pipes and fixtures.

A test tells you whether you need a softener for scale, a filter for taste and odor, or neither. It also keeps you from paying for equipment you do not need.

Softener, Filter or Reverse Osmosis?

They solve different problems.

SystemWhat it targetsGood fit for
Water softenerHardness minerals that cause scaleHomes with scale on fixtures and appliances
Whole-house carbon filterChlorine taste and odorHouseholds that dislike the taste of treated water
Reverse osmosis at the sinkMany dissolved substances at one tapDrinking and cooking water
Sediment filterParticles and rust flakesHomes with older pipes

What Hard Water and Filters Cannot Do

Softeners swap hardness minerals for sodium or potassium, which reduces scale. They do not purify water or remove most contaminants. Filters remove particular things and need regular changes.

No system fixes every concern. Decide what problem you want to solve, then match the equipment to it. A plumber can explain the limits and help you avoid buying a system that does not match your goal.

Older Pipes and Water Quality

A 2025 University of Illinois Chicago study says much of Lansing's older housing was built with lead plumbing and that the village is working with the Illinois EPA on lead service line replacement. A softener or carbon filter does not remove lead unless it is certified for it.

If lead is a concern, ask the village about its service line inventory and see water line repair and replacement. Nearby municipal reports advise flushing a tap for 30 seconds to two minutes if water has sat for hours.
