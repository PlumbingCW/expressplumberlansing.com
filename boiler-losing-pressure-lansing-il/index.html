Boiler Losing Pressure in Lansing, IL

A boiler that keeps losing pressure usually has a water leak somewhere in the system, a failed expansion tank or a relief valve that is letting water out. Topping up the pressure only helps for a while if the cause remains.

This guide explains the common causes, what you can check safely and when to call a plumber. It is written for homeowners in Lansing, IL with a hot water boiler.

What Boiler Pressure Is and Why It Matters

A hot water boiler is a closed loop filled with water. A gauge shows the pressure, and many systems run in a modest range when cold and a little higher when hot. Check your manual for the range.

If the pressure falls too low, the boiler may shut down. If it climbs too high, the relief valve opens to protect the system.

Common Causes of Pressure Loss

The pattern points to the cause.

What You Can Check Safely

These steps are safe for most homeowners.

1

Read the gauge

Compare it with the range in your manual.

2

Look for water

Check around the boiler, pipes and radiators for drips or stains.

3

Check the relief valve pipe

A wet or dripping discharge pipe points to the valve.

4

Call for service

If the pressure keeps dropping, call a plumber.

Why Fresh Water Is Not a Fix

Every time you add water to a closed heating system you add new oxygen and minerals. Oxygen accelerates rust in iron parts, and minerals can form scale on heat surfaces.

A system that needs frequent top-ups is losing water somewhere, and that water carries heat and the corrosion inhibitors you may have in the system. Finding the leak is worth more than the cost of the water.

Radiators, Valves and Hidden Leaks

Check radiator valves and the pipe joints beneath radiators for crusty deposits or stains. A slow leak at a valve stem can lose water for months without a puddle because the water evaporates from the hot surface.

If the boiler is in a basement, check the floor beneath it and the pipes overhead. A plumber can pressure test the system and use a camera or a moisture meter to find leaks that hide in walls and floors.

Safety Notes

Do not keep adding water to a boiler that is losing pressure without finding the cause, since fresh water carries oxygen that speeds corrosion. Never cap or block a relief valve.

If you smell gas, leave the house and call 911. Install a carbon monoxide alarm near sleeping areas and test it regularly.

Signs the Expansion Tank May Be Failing

The tank absorbs pressure changes as the water heats.

  • ✓
    Pressure rises sharply when the boiler firesThe tank is not absorbing the expansion.
  • ✓
    The relief valve drips after heatingPressure is exceeding its limit.
  • ✓
    The tank is heavy or waterloggedThe air side has failed.
  • ✓
    Frequent top-upsThe loss may be through the valve, not a pipe leak.

Why Annual Service Helps

A yearly service visit checks the pressure, the expansion tank and the relief valve before the heating season. Catching a weak tank early is cheaper than an emergency in January.
