How to Repair a Leaking Sink Faucet in Lansing, IL

A leaking sink faucet is usually a worn cartridge, washer, O-ring or valve seat, and many drips can be fixed in under an hour. Turn off the water under the sink, take the faucet apart, replace the worn part with an exact match and reassemble it.

This guide explains how to find the cause, how the repair goes and when it is time to call a plumber or replace the faucet. It is written for homeowners in Lansing, IL.

The Repair in Five Steps

The parts differ, but the order is the same.

1

Shut off the water

Close both shutoff valves under the sink and open the faucet to release pressure.

2

Remove the handle

Pry off the cap, remove the screw and lift the handle.

3

Pull the cartridge or stem

Take out the part that controls flow and note its shape.

4

Replace worn parts

Match the cartridge, washer or O-ring exactly, and clean the seat.

5

Reassemble and test

Turn the water on slowly and check for drips at the spout and base.

Which Part Causes Which Leak

Where the water appears points to the part.

Where it leaksLikely partFix
Spout drips when offCartridge, washer or seatReplace the part
Around the handleO-ring or packingReplace the seal
At the base of the spoutSpout O-ringsReplace the O-rings
Under the sinkSupply line or shutoff valveReplace the line or valve

Fix It Yourself or Call a Plumber

Many leaks are within reach of a homeowner.

You can often handle

  • A drip from a cartridge or washer
  • Cleaning a clogged aerator
  • Replacing a simple supply line

Call a plumber for

  • Corroded shutoff valves that will not close
  • A leak inside the wall or cabinet
  • A faucet whose body is cracked or corroded. See faucet and sink repair

A few items make the job smooth.

A Clogged Aerator Is Not a Leak

If flow is weak or sprays sideways, the aerator at the tip may be clogged with minerals or debris. Unscrew it, soak it in vinegar and rinse it.

Because we could not find a published hardness figure for Lansing water, we cannot say how fast minerals build up in your faucet. Test your tap and ask the village for its water quality report, and clean the aerator whenever flow changes.

Lansing, IL Note: What a Drip Costs on the Water Bill

The 2025 University of Illinois Chicago water affordability study for the Village of Lansing lists a first-block water rate of $9.16 per 1,000 gallons, with billing every quarter and sewer charges based on usage. As an example, a faucet losing one gallon an hour wastes about 8,760 gallons a year, which is roughly $80 in water charges at that rate before sewer charges.

The one gallon an hour figure is an illustration and your drip may be faster or slower. A faucet that drips fast enough to fill a cup in a minute wastes far more, and the repair is cheaper than the water.

When to Replace the Faucet Instead

If the faucet is old, corroded or no longer sold, parts can be hard to find, and a new faucet may be the better value. A new faucet also lets you upgrade to a style that suits your kitchen and a model that saves water.

A plumber can fit a new faucet and replace the supply lines and shutoff valves at the same visit. See Calumet City and Glenwood for nearby service areas.

A Word About Old Shutoff Valves

In older homes the shutoff valves under the sink can be stuck or brittle. Turning them hard can break them and cause a leak you did not have before.

If a valve will not turn easily, stop and call a plumber. Replacing worn valves is a short job and gives you a shutoff that works when you need it. A 2025 study found 39 percent of Lansing homes date from 1959 or earlier, so original valves are common.
