What the 2023 Floods Taught Calumet City Homeowners

ABC7 Chicago reported that the governor declared Calumet City a disaster area in July 2023 after heavy rain on July 5, and the Lansing Journal reported flash flooding on September 17 with the National Weather Service estimating 7 to 9 inches of rain. The mayor declared a state of emergency on September 20.

ABC7 also reported that residents with sump pumps and check valves still took in sewer water, and that city leaders said flooding came mainly from sewer backups. The city offered help covering about half the cost of added sewer improvements at homes.

If you want to reduce your risk, talk to a plumber about a backwater valve and a battery backup for your sump pump. See sump pump repair. Check your insurance too, since the same report said one resident's flood policy excluded water backup through drains.

Water, Levees and Lead

Calumet City's 2024 water report says it draws 100 percent of its water from Lake Michigan, 20 percent supplied by Chicago and 80 percent by Hammond. The city and Lansing also share a Little Calumet River levee restoration plan costing about $15.6 million, with about two miles of levee in Calumet City.

The city's report refers readers to a Lead Service Line Material Inventory on its website. If you want to check your own line, see water line repair and replacement.
