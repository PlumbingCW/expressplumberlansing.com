Garbage Disposal Installation in Lansing, IL

Installing a garbage disposal means mounting a grinder under the sink drain, connecting it to the drain pipe and wiring it to power. A disposal is a good fit for a kitchen with a solid drain line and a convenient outlet, and a poor fit if the drain is already prone to clogs.

This guide explains what to check before you buy, how installation works and how to keep a disposal running well. It is written for homeowners in Lansing, IL.

What You Need Before Buying

Check the sink: a disposal fits a standard drain opening of the kind most kitchen sinks have. Check under the sink for space, since the unit hangs below the drain and needs room around it.

Check power: a disposal needs an outlet or a hardwired connection and a switch. A dishwasher also connects to the disposal in many kitchens, so the plumber checks that connection.

How Installation Goes

It is a short job when the sink is in good shape.

1

Disconnect the old parts

Power is turned off and any old disposal or drain strainer is removed.

2

Mount the unit

The mounting ring and gasket are fixed under the sink.

3

Connect the drain

The discharge pipe and dishwasher line are connected.

4

Wire and test

The unit is wired or plugged in and tested for leaks.

Noise, Power and Placement

A larger or better insulated disposal makes less noise, but it also costs more. If your kitchen is open to a living area, noise may matter more than raw grinding power.

Check where the switch will go. A wall switch is convenient, but running wiring through a wall adds work. An air switch on the countertop can be easier to add in some kitchens.

Lansing, IL Note: Older Kitchen Drains

A 2025 University of Illinois Chicago study found that 39 percent of homes in Lansing were built in 1959 or earlier, and the Lansing Journal describes a postwar building boom. Kitchen drains in houses from those years may be galvanized steel or cast iron, which collect scale and slow down over decades.

A disposal sends more solids into that drain, so ask the plumber to check the line first. If the kitchen drain is slow now, see drain cleaning before adding a disposal.

Habits That Protect the Drain

Run cold water while the disposal works and for several seconds after, to flush scraps down the line. Feed scraps in small amounts rather than a heap.

Keep fat, oil, coffee grounds, eggshells and fibrous scraps out of the unit. They can build up in the line even if the disposal grinds them.

Disposal or Compost Bin?

Neither is right for everyone.

A disposal suits

  • Small food scraps from cooking
  • A household with a solid drain line
  • People who want fewer trash odors

A compost bin suits

  • Fibrous and bulky scraps
  • Households with a slow or old drain
  • Anyone who wants to reduce water use

When to Call a Plumber Instead of Doing It Yourself

Replacing a like-for-like disposal is a job many homeowners manage. A first-time install means new wiring, a new switch and possibly new drain parts, and mistakes can cause leaks or electrical hazards.

A plumber can also check that the sink drain and trap are in good shape. See garbage disposal repair if you already have one, and our Glenwood and Lynwood pages for nearby service.
