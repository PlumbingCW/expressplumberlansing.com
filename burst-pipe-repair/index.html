Burst Pipe: The First Five Minutes

Turn off the main water shutoff. It is usually where the water line enters the house, often near the meter in a basement. Turn it clockwise until it stops.

Switch off power to any wet area at the breaker if you can reach it safely. Open the lowest faucet to drain the remaining water, and move valuables away from the wet area. Then call the 24/7 line.

What the Plumber Does

A repair follows a set order.

1

Confirm the shutoff

The plumber checks that the main is closed and the line is drained.

2

Find the break

The damaged section is found, and nearby pipe is checked for more damage.

3

Cut and replace

The bad section is cut out and replaced with new pipe and fittings.

4

Pressure test

Water is turned back on slowly and the repair is checked for leaks.

After the Repair: Drying and Checking

Once the line is fixed and the water is back on, check the area for hidden moisture. Wet drywall, insulation and carpet can hold water for days and start to grow mold if they stay damp.

Run fans, open windows when the weather allows and consider a dehumidifier. If walls or flooring stayed wet for more than a day or two, a water damage specialist can check for moisture behind the surface.

Winter Freezes in the Chicago Southland

Lansing winters can drop well below freezing, and pipes in unheated spaces such as garages, crawlspaces and exterior walls are the ones that burst. A 2025 study found that 39 percent of local homes date from 1959 or earlier, and older houses often have less insulation around pipes.

Keep cabinet doors open on cold nights under sinks on outside walls, let a faucet drip during a hard freeze and know where the main shutoff is. A frozen pipe that has not burst yet is still worth a call, since thawing it the wrong way can crack it.
