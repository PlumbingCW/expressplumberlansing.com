What the County Plan Says About Oakwood Estates

Cook County's multi-jurisdictional hazard mitigation plan includes a Lansing annex. It says the Oakwood Estates subdivision is vulnerable to flooding from local creeks, and that basements are at risk of flooding in many areas of the village.

The plan does not say how many Oakwood homes have flooded, what kind of sewers serve the subdivision or which supplier provides its water. We do not guess at any of those. What the plan gives you is a good reason to take basement protection seriously.

Flood Facts From the Same Plan

The annex reports 15 FEMA-identified repetitive loss properties in Lansing, 14 of them single-family homes. It also lists federal declarations for severe storms and flooding on September 13, 2008 and July 19, 2010. These are village-wide figures, not counts for Oakwood Estates.

The plan says the Village sends floodplain letters to residents each year and also issues flyers on floodplains and insurance requirements. Ask your insurer what your policy covers, since flood and sewer backup are often handled separately from a standard homeowner policy. We are plumbers, not insurance advisers.

Plumbing Checks for a Creek-Side Subdivision

Each of these is general advice for homes in flood-prone areas.

    Test the sump pumpPour water into the pit and watch the pump start, run and shut off. See sump pump repair if it does not.
  • ✓
    Check seepage at the walls and floorDamp patches after rain suggest groundwater, and basement waterproofing covers the fixes.
  • ✓
    Watch the floor drainsA gurgle or a smell during heavy rain can mean the sewer is backing up, and sewer line cleaning clears the line.
  • ✓
    Raise the water heater and furnaceEquipment on the floor is the first to fail in a flood. Ask about water heater repair if your tank sits low.
  • ✓
    Keep drains movingSlow drains before a storm are easier to fix than a backup during one, and drain cleaning is the usual first step.

Before the Next Heavy Storm

Ten minutes of preparation can save a lot of cleanup.

Check the discharge outlet

Make sure the pipe that carries pump water outside is clear and points away from the house.

Lift stored items

Put boxes and electronics on shelves rather than on the floor.

Know your shutoffs

Find the main water valve and the electrical panel before you need them.

How to Check Your Own Flood Risk

This page rests on a county plan, not on a plumber's inspection of each street, so confirm your own situation. Ask the Village whether your property sits in a mapped floodplain, and look up your address on FEMA's flood map service.

A plumber can then match the protection to the risk, whether that is a better pump, a backup power source or a backwater valve on the sewer line.
